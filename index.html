Opening-weekend audience research

Read the crowd before the box office does.

The person who writes a review already knows how they feel. The studio reading 20,000 posts, forum threads and survey answers doesn't, and none of them come with a star rating. Crowd Read scores every comment, tells you which parts of the film people love or hate, and puts the comments that need an answer at the top.

      How it works

      Eight models tried, one kept

      The model comes from the course's algorithm-harness notebook: 50,000 IMDB reviews, cleaned and turned into numbers, then used to train and grade eight classifiers on the same 10,000 held-out reviews.

      1. CleanLowercase; strip HTML tags, links, punctuation and numbers.clean_text()
      2. TokenizeSplit each review into words.nltk.word_tokenize
      3. Drop stop wordsRemove filler words like "the" and "of".stopwords.words('english')
      4. LemmatizeReduce words to dictionary form: "reviewers" → "reviewer".WordNetLemmatizer
      5. TF-IDFKeep the 5,000 most frequent words; weight each by how distinctive it is.TfidfVectorizer(max_features=5000)
      6. ClassifyAdd up a learned weight per word and turn the total into a probability.LogisticRegression

      Why logistic regression

      It won on every metric: 88.5% accuracy and 0.887 F1, ahead of CatBoost, LightGBM and XGBoost. It also trains in seconds and every prediction can be explained word by word, which matters when someone asks why a comment was flagged.

      Measured from its probabilities rather than its yes/no answers, its ROC-AUC is 0.957. (The notebook computes ROC-AUC from 0/1 predictions, which gives 0.885.)

      The trained weights, all 5,000 of them, ship with this page as a 200 KB file, so it scores comments without a server.

      Who it's for

      People who read a crowd, not a review

      Studio marketing

      By Sunday night, see whether the cast or the visuals are carrying the film and cut week two's trailer around it. If the ending is what people complain about, stop teasing it.

      Theater programmers

      Decide which titles keep their big screens for a second week from how local audiences talk about them, not only from ticket counts.

      Streaming and sequels

      Before greenlighting season two, find out which parts viewers keep praising and which they keep calling slow or confusing.

      Community and social teams

      Start the day with the clearly negative comments at the top of the queue, and hand the "can't decide" ones to a person.

      Limits

      Where it gets it wrong

      Trust the totals more than any single verdict. About one comment in nine is misread.

      "Not" disappears

      NLTK's stop-word list includes not, so the pipeline turns "not good" into "good". In the sample, "Mara Quill is brilliant. The rest of the movie is not." scores as positive.

      Trained on long reviews

      IMDB reviews average about 230 words. Short posts give the model only a few words to go on, so their scores drift toward the middle.

      Only two answers

      The model knows positive and negative. Neutral or off-topic comments still get pushed to one side; the "read these yourself" list catches some of them.

      Sarcasm and contrast

      "Great acting can't save a script this weak" is a word count to the model, not an argument. Clause-level scoring helps with "but", not with irony.
